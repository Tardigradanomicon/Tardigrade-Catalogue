Tardigrade Anatomy

A guide to the structures and anatomical terminology used throughout the catalogue.

01

Body Plan

Tardigrades are bilaterally symmetrical microscopic animals with a distinct head region and four pairs of lobopodous legs. The first three pairs of legs are associated with the trunk, while the fourth pair occurs toward the posterior end of the body.

The overall appearance of the body varies considerably among tardigrade groups. Features of the cuticle, appendages, feeding apparatus, and legs are especially important in tardigrade taxonomy.

Region Description
Head Anterior region bearing the mouth and, in some groups, sensory appendages and eyespots.
Trunk Main body region associated with the first three pairs of legs.
Posterior region Caudal region associated with the fourth pair of legs.
Legs Four pairs of lobopodous appendages, usually terminating in claws and, in some marine tardigrades, digits.
02

Cuticle

The cuticle forms the outer covering of the tardigrade body and is periodically shed during molting. Its appearance ranges from relatively smooth surfaces to highly structured or plated forms.

Plates

Cuticular plates are hardened regions of the cuticle that occur in some tardigrades, particularly within several heterotardigrade groups. Their number, arrangement, and sculpture can provide important taxonomic characters.

Surface structures

Depending on the species, the cuticle may bear structures such as spines, papillae, filaments, or other projections.

03

Head & Cephalic Structures

The head contains the mouth and may bear a variety of sensory structures. These structures are particularly important when identifying many heterotardigrades.

Cirri

Slender sensory appendages associated with the head and, in some taxa, other regions of the body.

Clavae

Sensory structures occurring in various heterotardigrades. Their presence, absence, position, and form can be useful taxonomic characters.

Eyespots

Simple eyespots may occur on the head in some tardigrades. Their presence or absence can be recorded as an identification character.

04

Bucco-pharyngeal Apparatus

The bucco-pharyngeal apparatus is the feeding apparatus extending from the mouth toward the pharynx. Its structures are among the most important morphological characters used in tardigrade taxonomy.

Structure Description
Mouth The anterior opening through which food enters the feeding apparatus.
Buccal tube A cuticular tube connecting the mouth region with the pharyngeal apparatus.
Stylets Paired piercing structures associated with feeding.
Stylet sheaths Cuticular structures surrounding the stylets.
Stylet supports Structures associated with the stylet apparatus and its connection to the buccal region.
Pharyngeal bulb Muscular region of the feeding apparatus that generates suction during feeding.
Placoid apparatus Cuticular structures within the pharyngeal apparatus. Their arrangement and form vary among major tardigrade groups.
Why it matters for identification

The bucco-pharyngeal apparatus can differ substantially among tardigrade groups. Characters such as the buccal tube, stylets, stylet supports, and pharyngeal structures are therefore frequently recorded in species descriptions.

05

Legs & Claws

Tardigrades possess four pairs of lobopodous legs. Leg morphology is one of the most important sources of taxonomic information in the group.

Character What to examine
Leg pairs Legs I–IV and their relative position on the body.
Claws Number, arrangement, relative size, branching, and accessory structures.
Accessory points Additional pointed structures associated with some claws.
Spurs Basal or other projections associated with certain claw types.
Leg appendages Some species possess spines, papillae, or other sensory structures associated with the legs.
Digits Finger- or toe-like distal structures occurring in some marine tardigrades. They should not be assumed to be present in all tardigrades.
06

Internal Anatomy

Digestive system

The digestive tract extends from the mouth through the pharyngeal region and into the intestine.

Musculature

Tardigrades lack an internal bony skeleton. Muscles attach to the body wall and operate the body and legs.

Nervous system

The nervous system includes a dorsal brain and a ventral nerve cord associated with segmental ganglia.

Reproductive structures

Reproductive anatomy varies among tardigrades and may include structures such as the gonopore and reproductive ducts.

07

Anatomy & Identification

Not every anatomical structure is present in every tardigrade. Identification therefore depends on comparing the characters that are actually present, absent, or variable within the relevant taxonomic group.

The catalogue uses these anatomical terms throughout individual species accounts. For a practical guide to using morphological characters for identification, see the Identification page.
