Key diagnostic characters

Anisonyches diakidius is characterized by the presence of primary clavae, four claws on legs I–III and three claws on leg IV, and a papilla near the base of the fourth leg pair. Legs I–III lack spines and papillae.

Color & Cuticle

Color

Transparent.

Cuticle

Smooth, without plates, ornamentation, or evident punctations.

The body is described as worm-like.

Eyes

Black, ovoid eyes are present.

Bucco-pharyngeal apparatus
  • Mouth terminal.
  • Buccal tube thin, approximately 24.0 µm.
  • Stylets approximately 31.6 µm, entering through prominent sheaths.
  • Pharyngeal bulb small.
  • Three placoids, approximately 6 µm.
Plates

Plates absent.

The cuticle lacks ornamentation and evident punctations.

Appendages

Head

  • Head conical with terminal mouth.
  • Cephalic cirri have swollen, transparent bases with short flagella.
  • Median cephalic cirrus very short (2.5 µm).
  • Primary clavae ovoid, approximately 3.6 µm.

Lateral

  • Lateral cirrus A short (4.2 µm) and slightly anterior to the primary clavae.
  • Secondary clavae present as small bulges.

Dorsal

  • Cirrus E approximately 5.5 µm.
  • Located dorso-anterior to leg IV.
Legs & Claws

Legs

  • Legs stubby and approximately equal in length.
  • Legs I–III lack spines and papillae.
  • A papilla approximately 3.7 µm is present dorsally near the base of leg IV.

Claws

  • Legs I–III possess four claws per leg.
  • Claws on legs I–III progressively increase in length from inner to outer claws.
  • Leg IV possesses three claws.
  • Leg IV claws are approximately equal in length (≈8 µm).
  • Claws are slightly curved.
  • Basal spurs are widely divergent and approximately horizontal.
  • Accessory points occur on the medial claws of legs I–III and the inner two claws of leg IV.

06

Taxonomic Notes

Anisonyches diakidius is distinguished by the combination of four claws on legs I–III, three claws on leg IV, primary clavae, and a papilla near the base of the fourth leg pair.

Legs I–III lack spines and papillae. The claws of legs I–III progressively increase in length from the inner to outer claws, whereas the claws of leg IV are approximately equal in length.

The basal spurs are widely divergent and approximately horizontal.
