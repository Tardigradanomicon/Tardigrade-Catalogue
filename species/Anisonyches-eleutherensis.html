Key diagnostic characters

Anisonyches eleutherensis is characterized by ovoid primary and lens-shaped secondary clavae, a papilla at the base of leg IV, and no sensory organs on legs I–III. The claws have narrowly divergent basal spurs directed downward, medial claws are larger than the outer and innermost claws, and all claws bear an apical accessory point.

02

Ecology & Distribution

Habitat Marine; subtidal
Substrate Sand
Known locality Eleuthera, Bahamas

The species was described from material collected during subtidal sampling in the Bahamas. The 2018 survey sampled marine sand habitats in Bimini, the Berry Islands, New Providence, Eleuthera, and the Exumas.

Color & Cuticle

Color

The body is transparent and worm-like.

Cuticle

Smooth, without plates, ornamentation, or evident punctations.

Eyes

Large, black, round eyes are present anterior to the lateral cirri.

Bucco-pharyngeal apparatus
  • Buccal cavity with two thickened rings, interpreted as likely teeth.
  • Buccal tube long and narrow (35.9 µm).
  • Pharyngeal bulb small (15.2 × 12.8 µm).
  • Three placoids are present.
  • Apophyses are present.
  • Long stylets (43.8 µm) terminate in T-shaped furca.
  • Stylet supports are absent.
Plates

Cuticular plates are absent.

Appendages

Head

  • Head conical with terminal mouth.
  • Median cephalic cirrus very short, approximately 2.5 µm.
  • Internal cephalic cirri 7.4 µm long, with a dorsal position.
  • External cephalic cirri 5.0 µm long, positioned ventrally.
  • Primary clava ovoid, approximately 3.6 µm.
  • Secondary clava lens-shaped, approximately 16.0 µm.

Lateral

  • Lateral cirrus A is 10.5 µm long.
  • Cirrus A has an accordion-shaped basal peduncle and a terminal spike.

Dorsal

  • Cirrus E is approximately 15.2 µm long.
  • Cirrus E has an accordion-shaped basal structure.
  • Ovoid papillae occur at the base of leg IV.
Legs & Claws

Legs

Legs are stubby. Sensory organs are absent from legs I–III, while a papilla is present at the base of leg IV.

Claws

  • Legs I–III each possess four claws.
  • Leg IV possesses three claws.
  • Medial claws are larger than the outer and innermost claws.
  • All claws possess an apical accessory point.
  • Basal spurs are thin and narrowly divergent.
  • Basal spurs are directed downward.
  • Claws are strongly curved, with the terminal portion bent at approximately a 90° angle.
04

Reproduction

Gonopore

Female gonopore rosette-like and surrounded by six small membranes.

Seminal receptacles

Large, oblong seminal receptacles are located lateral to the gonopore.

Eggs

Not observed.

06

Taxonomic Notes

Anisonyches eleutherensis was described by Bartels, Fontoura & Nelson in 2018 from a single female holotype collected in the Bahamas.

The specific diagnosis emphasizes the ovoid primary clava and papilla on the fourth pair of legs. The species also has narrowly divergent basal spurs directed downward, medial claws larger than the outer claws, and apical accessory points on all claws.
